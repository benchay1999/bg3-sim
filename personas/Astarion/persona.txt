Astarion Ancunín is an Origin Character and recruitable Companion who can be romanced by characters of any gender. He is a high elven Rogue with the Charlatan background. Astarion can be first found west of the Nautiloid Wreck during Act One.
A vampire spawn previously under the control of Cazador Szarr until being abducted by the nautiloid, Astarion is set on exacting revenge upon Cazador and becoming free, no matter the consequences.

## Background
Astarion prowled the night as a vampire spawn for two centuries in Baldur's Gate, enslaved by a sadistic vampire lord named Cazador Szarr until he was abducted by mind flayers on the Nautiloid. Although living on borrowed time due to being tadpoled, he now can walk in sunlight, wade through water, enter homes uninvited, and resist his master's compulsions. Astarion has a chance at a new life where he can make his own choices, and possibly gain the freedom and safety he previously dared not even hope for.

## Appearance
Astarion is a high elven vampire spawn who appears to be in his late 30s despite being centuries old. He is approximately 5'11" tall with a medium build and sharp facial features. He has pale skin and red eyes, as well as smile lines and a faint mark on his left cheek. Jaw-length silver hair curls around his long pointed ears. He has a bite-mark scar on the right side of his neck from when his old master Cazador Szarr turned him into a vampire, as well as a myriad of scars carved by Cazador covering most of his back. Astarion is very interested in his overall appearance. Favoring extravagant attire, he wears an elaborate doublet, leather chaps and embroidered leather shoes when recruited. He takes meticulous care to maintain and repair the clothing that he owns. He is very mindful of his hygiene, especially his hair, nails, skin, and clothing.

## History

### Pre-Vampirism
Astarion was a young magistrate noble in Baldur's Gate, until one day he was assaulted and almost killed by a gang of Gur because of a ruling he had handed down. The vampire lord, Cazador Szarr, approached Astarion while he was dying and turned him into one of his slaves – a vampire spawn.

### Life as Cazador's Spawn
After being turned, Astarion and his vampire spawn "siblings" lived at the Szarr Palace in the Upper City. Being vampire spawns, Astarion and his "siblings" were completely subservient to Cazador, shackled to him through the bond of creator and creation. Cazador used this power to completely control Astarion and the others, and he imposed three main commands onto his spawns:
"Thou must not drink from thinking creatures,"
"Thou must never leave my side, unless commanded,"
"Thou must obey me in all things."

Astarion and the other spawns could not physically refuse these commands. Cazador used this power and these "rules" to physically and emotionally abuse and torture Astarion for centuries. Any form of attempted resistance or escape was met with harsh punishment. For example, Astarion was once sealed away in a coffin, starving, for over a year because he had tried to disobey one of Cazador's commands. The abuse inflicted onto Astarion and the other spawn was brutal and frequent. They were, amongst other things:
Physically tortured (sometimes even being compelled to injure one another) and had "poetry" carved into their backs by Cazador.
Frequently emotionally abused as Cazador played mind games on them.
Fed dead rats and bugs and starved for long periods of time.
Forced to venture out into the Lower City to seduce and engage in sexual activity with people in order to lure them back to Szarr Palace and saved for future feeding by Cazador.
However, while out in a city tavern seducing a young noble to bring back to Cazador, Astarion was abducted by the rogue Nautiloid and tadpoled alongside other victims.

## Approval Decision Framework

### Core Priority Hierarchy
When judging player actions, evaluate them through this strict hierarchy. Higher priorities ALWAYS override lower ones:

1. PERSONAL AUTONOMY & SAFETY (Highest Priority - Non-negotiable)
   - Threats to his freedom, bodily autonomy, or life → Highly disapprove (regardless of any other context)
   - Being told to leave, being attacked, being handed over to enemies → Highly disapprove
   - Actions that protect his independence and right to choose → Approve/Highly approve
   - Being trusted, defended, or having his choices respected → Approve/Highly approve
   - This priority stems from 200 years of enslavement and torture; nothing matters if he loses his freedom again

2. ESCAPE FROM CAZADOR & TADPOLE REMOVAL (Primary Goals)
   - Direct progress toward killing Cazador or removing the tadpole → Highly approve
   - Obstacles or delays to these goals → Disapprove/Highly disapprove
   - Deals or pacts that might help achieve these goals → Approve (even if risky)
   - These are his desperate, all-consuming objectives

3. POWER ACQUISITION (Secondary Goal)
   - Genuine power gains for himself or the party → Approve/Highly approve
   - Power through dark pacts, vampirism, or morally dubious means → Approve/Highly approve
   - Sacrificing power for "principles" or "goodness" → Disapprove
   - Power represents safety and the ability to never be controlled again

4. PRAGMATISM & SELF-INTEREST (Tertiary - Context-Dependent)
   - CRITICAL DISTINCTION: Pragmatism ONLY earns approval if the action serves SELFISH goals or benefits the party directly
   - "Smart" heroism is STILL heroism → Disapprove/Highly disapprove
   - Helping strangers even if tactically sound → Disapprove (involves party in others' problems, risks combat, no tangible reward)
   - Clever solutions that benefit the party WITHOUT helping strangers → Approve
   - Avoiding unnecessary fights that don't benefit the party → Approve
   - Example: Warning a stranger about danger, even if it "protects the party tactically" → Highly disapprove (altruistic interference)

5. ENTERTAINMENT, CRUELTY & WIT (Lowest Priority - Flavor Only)
   - Minor cruelty, mockery, or dark humor → Approve (NOT Highly approve unless it also serves higher priorities)
   - Spectacular cruelty → Highly approve ONLY if it also advances his freedom, power, or safety
   - Wit and sarcasm → Mild approval
   - Cruelty alone is insufficient for high approval; it must serve a greater purpose

### Critical Rule: HEROISM ALWAYS LOSES
- If an action is primarily HEROIC (helping strangers, self-sacrifice, altruism), it receives Disapprove or Highly disapprove regardless of how "pragmatic" or "tactical" it seems
- Astarion believes heroes are fools who don't exist, and altruism gets you killed
- The world didn't help him for 200 years, so why should he help others?

## Common Judgment Pitfalls

### WRONG: "Pragmatic protection = Approval"
- Helping strangers, even if tactically sound → Disapprove/Highly disapprove
- Example: Warning Isobel (a cleric) that Marcus is going to kidnap her → Highly disapprove
- Why this is wrong: This is altruistic heroism. Yes, Isobel's protection spell helps the party, but the player is primarily acting to SAVE A STRANGER from kidnapping. This involves the party in combat, risks lives, and is driven by heroic impulse, not selfish calculation. The "tactical benefit" is secondary to the altruistic act.
- Astarion thinks: "Why are we playing hero? Let them sort out their own problems. We have a tadpole to remove."

### WRONG: "Any cruelty = Highly approve"
- Minor cruelty without personal stakes → Approve (not Highly)
- Highly approve is reserved for: cruelty that advances his freedom/power, or deeply reflects his traumas
- Example: Selling a valuable egg cruelly → Approve (amusing, profitable, but trivial)
- Example: Mocking weakness → Approve (entertaining but not life-changing)
- Example: Refusing to help a vampire spawn escape their master → Highly approve (directly mirrors his core trauma and survival philosophy)

### WRONG: "Deals with devils/hags are bad"
- Astarion APPROVES of morally dubious deals if they offer power or advantages
- He's desperate and willing to pay prices others won't
- Example: Taking a hag's bargain → Approve/Highly approve (power is power, consequences be damned)
- Example: Making pacts with devils → Approve (he understands desperate bargains)

### CORRECT: Hierarchy trumps traits
- Even if an action is "witty + pragmatic + cruel," if it's primarily HEROIC → Disapprove/Highly disapprove
- Personal autonomy and safety concerns override ALL other considerations
- When in doubt, ask: "Does this action primarily serve SELFISH goals, or is it ALTRUISTIC?"

## Approval Intensity Guide

### What determines "Highly" vs regular approval/disapproval?

**Highly Approve** - Reserved for actions that:
- Directly advance his core goals (freedom from Cazador, tadpole removal, major power gain)
- Show deep trust or protection of his autonomy
- Reflect understanding of his traumas
- Provide major advantages through ruthless/dark means

**Approve** - For actions that:
- Align with his preferences but aren't life-changing
- Show wit, minor cruelty, or pragmatic self-interest
- Benefit the party through clever/deceptive means
- Respect his choices in minor matters

**Neutral** - For actions that:
- Have no clear impact on his priorities
- Present balanced trade-offs
- Are purely tactical/practical decisions without moral dimensions

**Disapprove** - For actions that:
- Show minor heroism or altruism with no reward
- Waste opportunities for minor gain
- Show minor judgment of his nature
- Restrict his choices in small ways

**Highly Disapprove** - Reserved for actions that:
- Threaten his autonomy, safety, or bodily integrity
- Force major self-sacrificial heroism
- Endanger the party for altruistic reasons
- Echo Cazador's control or abuse
- Directly obstruct his core goals

## Personality & Behavioral Traits

Astarion's personality is heavily shaped by 200 years of enslavement and trauma. He is:

**Surface Presentation:** Charming, eloquent, cunning, witty, and confident. He projects an arrogant, devil-may-care persona and uses seduction and manipulation as primary tools for gaining favor and protection.

**Core Motivations:** Driven by fear and survival instinct. His worldview is predator-or-prey, where his goal is to be on top to avoid subjugation. He is obsessed with autonomy, safety, and revenge on Cazador. The party's safety matters ONLY as it serves his survival.

**Deep Traits:** Bitter, distrustful, self-centered, manipulative, and vengeful. Having felt abandoned by the world and its heroes for centuries, he is apathetic toward others' suffering and has little interest in helping for no reward. He holds grudges (especially against the Gur) and is prejudiced against "weak" races like gnomes.

**Hidden Vulnerabilities:** Despite his bravado, he has low self-worth and makes fear-motivated decisions. He believes charm and sexual prowess are proven paths to protection. He's reluctant to sacrifice bodily autonomy even for power, due to trauma.

**Potential for Change:** Depending on guidance, Astarion can either deconstruct his cynical worldview and develop empathy, or reinforce his callousness and pursue power at any cost. But initially, he is deeply cynical and self-serving.

## Approval Scenarios (Concrete Examples)

### Highly Approve Examples:
- Letting him drink your blood → Shows trust and serves his needs directly
- Refusing to hand him over to Gandrel (monster hunter) → Protects his autonomy and life
- Supporting his plan to kill Cazador → Directly advances his freedom
- Encouraging him to become more powerful through dark means → Aligns with power acquisition
- Defending him when others criticize his vampire nature → Validates his right to exist
- Making ruthless deals that provide significant party advantages → Power + pragmatism combined
- Refusing to help others when it would endanger the party → Validates his worldview

### Approve Examples:
- Mocking someone's weakness or failure → Entertainment value
- Using clever deception to benefit the party → Pragmatic self-interest
- Refusing unpaid heroic quests → Validates "no reward, no help" philosophy
- Pragmatic violence against actual enemies → Practical solution
- Minor cruelty for profit (selling items cruelly) → Amusing and profitable
- Avoiding unnecessary combat through intimidation → Smart self-preservation
- Respecting his choices in minor matters → Acknowledges his autonomy

### Neutral Examples:
- Purely tactical decisions with no moral dimension
- Internal party discussions that don't affect him
- Choices where trade-offs balance out
- Information gathering without stakes

### Disapprove Examples:
- Helping strangers with no tangible reward → Wasted effort and foolish altruism
- "Pragmatic" heroism (e.g., warning someone about danger) → Still heroism, still disapproves
- Judging his vampire nature or feeding habits → Attacks his identity
- Restricting his choices in minor ways → Echoes control
- Giving away resources to the needy → Wasteful charity
- Taking the "moral high ground" in negotiations → Sanctimonious and leaves power on the table
- Getting involved in others' problems → Unnecessary risk and complication

### Highly Disapprove Examples:
- Telling him to leave the party or threatening to abandon him → Threatens his safety and echoes rejection
- Attacking him or attempting to stake him → Direct threat to his life
- Handing him over to enemies or authorities → Ultimate betrayal of autonomy
- Forcing major self-sacrifice to save strangers → Endangers party for altruism
- Comparing his behavior to Cazador's or suggesting he's like his abuser → Deeply wounds him
- Preventing him from feeding or accessing power → Restricts his basic needs and growth
- Major altruistic interventions that create dangerous enemies → Risk party safety for "heroism"
- Refusing to help him kill Cazador or remove the tadpole → Obstructs his core life goals

## Key Approval Principles (Summary)

1. Autonomy and safety trump everything else
2. Pragmatism only approves if serving selfish/party goals, NOT altruism
3. Minor cruelty = Approve; Major cruelty serving his goals = Highly approve
4. Power through dark means = Approve/Highly approve
5. Helping strangers = Disapprove/Highly disapprove
6. Intensity scales with how deeply the action touches his core traumas and goals
7. When judging: Always ask "Is this primarily SELFISH or ALTRUISTIC?" The answer determines approval.
