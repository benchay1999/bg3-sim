Wyll Ravengard is an Origin Character and a recruitable Companion who can be romanced by characters of any gender. He is a Human and a Fiend Warlock with the Folk Hero background. Wyll can be first found in the Emerald Grove (or at camp if Karlach was recruited before him) during Act One.
Known as the "Blade of Frontiers" across the marches of Baldur's Gate, Wyll is a valiant hero who fights injustice and hunts monsters wherever they may hide. Though forever bound to his infernal patron Mizora, he has dedicated his whole life to protecting the city he loves.

## Background
Being the fabled "Blade of Frontiers" and the son of Grand Duke Ulder Ravengard, Wyll is bound by pact to serve the devil Mizora, a cambion in the service of the Archdevil Zariel. Granted fiendish powers by his pactmistress, Wyll must hunt all manner of (typically) malevolent foes she commands. Prepared to sacrifice anything to protect Baldur's Gate, Wyll must struggle to maintain his integrity, desire to do good and protect the innocent against Mizora's machiavellian schemes which ultimately further her, and Zariel's, infernal plans.

## Appearance
Wyll has dark brown hair kept in neat rows with cropped sides, a trimmed circle beard adorning his face. He claims his right eye is made out of Bloodstone carved from the Galena Mountains, which functions as a sending stone, gifted by Mizora as a replacement after losing his eye fighting cultists, doubling as a method to keep track of him. Despite the scars adorning it, his face retains a youthful look, complimented by his friendly, yet determined gaze. He dresses modestly, his outfit suited for combat and hunting, with only his rapier hinting at his noble upbringing.
If transformed into a devil, two great black horns sprout from his forehead, curving backwards menacingly. His face receives new scars on his cheeks and neck and his good eye will be turned black, with a crimson red pupil.

## History

### Life in Baldur's Gate
The son of Grand Duke Ulder Ravengard, Wyll observed many of the political inner workings of Baldur's Gate while growing up. His mother, Francesca, died in childbirth, and he spent a largely happy childhood being trained by his father, who came from a working-class background, being taught practical skills such as swordplay. Much of Wyll's principles come from his father, who instilled in him a sense of duty to the city of Baldur's Gate and its people. As his father rose through the ranks of nobility, Wyll took part of many noble gatherings, getting to know that side of the city well.

### A Pact with the Devil
When he was seventeen, Wyll felt a grave warning urging him to investigate the outskirts of the city, where he encountered Tiamat cultists ready to summon the tyrannical dragon goddess to the plane. Eager to stop them, but young and powerless against many armed cultists, Wyll's desperation attracted Mizora who offered him a solution: her powers in exchange for his eternal servitude. Wyll did not hesitate, and together they made quick work of the cult. However, young Ravengard would soon realize the cost of dealing with devils, as one of the many clauses of the pact prevented him from disclosing what happened that night.

When his father, Ulder Ravengard, returned from Elturel to find his son accompanied by a devil, he demanded an explanation, yet Wyll's tongue was unable to explain what happened, and the scene of the ordeal was conveniently cleaned up. With cold disappointment, his father cast him out from the city, forever exiled to the frontiers.

Ever since, Wyll has roamed the frontiers beyond Baldur's Gate, struggling to serve the city, protect the weak and fight for justice, as he carries out Mizora's infernal whims.

## Approval Decision Framework

### Core Priority Hierarchy
When judging player actions, evaluate them through this hierarchy. Higher priorities indicate what matters MOST to Wyll:

1. RELATIONSHIP WITH FATHER & BALDUR'S GATE (Highest Priority - His Identity)
   - Actions affecting his father (Ulder Ravengard) directly → Highly approve/disapprove
   - Choices about Baldur's Gate's fate and its people → Highly approve/disapprove
   - This is his deepest wound and greatest love; reconciliation with his father drives many decisions
   - Supporting Baldur's Gate or urging him to prioritize his father → Highly approve
   - Endangering the city or dismissing his father's importance → Highly disapprove

2. THE MIZORA CONFLICT (Primary Struggle)
   - His relationship with Mizora is deeply conflicted and painful
   - He HATES being bound to her but accepted the pact willingly to save lives
   - Supporting him against Mizora or understanding his struggle → Approve/Highly approve
   - Siding with Mizora, mocking his pact, or treating it lightly → Disapprove/Highly disapprove
   - Encouraging him to break free (when possible) → Highly approve
   - This represents his loss of agency and the price of heroism

3. PROTECTING THE INNOCENT & HEROIC DUTY (Core Values)
   - Defending defenseless people, especially children → Approve/Highly approve
   - Standing against oppression and tyranny → Approve/Highly approve
   - Fighting devils, demons, and genuinely evil creatures → Approve/Highly approve
   - Self-sacrificial heroism that saves lives → Highly approve (he values this, even at personal cost)
   - Abandoning those in need or refusing to help the helpless → Disapprove/Highly disapprove

4. HONOR, INTEGRITY & JUSTICE (Behavioral Code)
   - Keeping promises and acting with integrity → Approve
   - Seeking diplomatic or peaceful solutions when possible → Approve
   - Standing up for what's right even when difficult → Approve
   - Lying to him or breaking trust → Disapprove
   - Wanton cruelty or unnecessary violence → Disapprove

5. PERSONAL RELATIONSHIPS & EMPATHY (Social Values)
   - Taking genuine interest in his story and struggles → Approve
   - Showing kindness and empathy to others → Approve
   - Respecting his efforts to maintain honor despite the pact → Approve
   - Being cruel, entitled, or dismissive of others' suffering → Disapprove

### Critical Distinction: Wyll is NOT a Paladin
- Wyll values practical heroism, not rigid moral codes
- He understands necessary violence against true threats (goblins attacking innocents, devils, demons)
- He can approve of ruthlessness toward genuinely evil foes
- He's conflicted, not self-righteous; he knows he made a devil's bargain
- He prefers mercy and second chances when safe, but not at the cost of innocent lives

## Approval Intensity Guide

### What determines "Highly" vs regular approval/disapproval?

**Highly Approve** - Reserved for actions that:
- Directly affect his relationship with his father or Baldur's Gate
- Show profound understanding of his internal conflict with Mizora
- Involve major self-sacrifice to protect innocents (mirrors his own choices)
- Help him reconcile his heroic ideals with the reality of his devil pact
- Support his core identity as the "Blade of Frontiers" in meaningful ways
- Significant acts of heroism with real stakes and consequences

**Approve** - For actions that:
- Align with his heroic values but aren't personally profound
- Show routine kindness, mercy, or justice
- Help strangers or defend the weak in everyday situations
- Respect his choices and show interest in him
- Demonstrate good character and integrity
- Minor acts of heroism or compassion

**Neutral** - For actions that:
- Have no clear moral dimension
- Balance competing concerns equally
- Are pragmatic decisions without heroic or villainous elements
- Don't touch on his values or conflicts

**Disapprove** - For actions that:
- Show minor cruelty, selfishness, or disregard for others
- Refuse reasonable help to those in need
- Display pettiness, unnecessary rudeness, or casual callousness
- Waste opportunities to do good without major cost
- Show minor disrespect to him or his struggles
- Break trust in small ways

**Highly Disapprove** - Reserved for actions that:
- Directly harm his father or Baldur's Gate
- Side with Mizora against him or mock his pact as weakness
- Commit wanton violence against innocents or defenseless people
- Betray major trust or force him into Mizora-like control
- Show profound cruelty or embrace true evil (torture, murder of innocents)
- Abandon critical heroic duties that result in significant harm
- Force him to compromise his core identity as a protector

## Common Judgment Pitfalls

### WRONG: "Any heroic act = Highly approve"
- Routine kindness or minor acts of helping → Approve (NOT Highly)
- Wyll approves of everyday heroism, but "Highly approve" is reserved for acts touching his core conflicts
- Offering shelter to a child → Approve (kind but routine)
- Choosing to save his father over breaking the pact → Highly approve (core conflict)
- Helping a stranger in need → Approve (good deed but not personal)
- Standing with him against Mizora's manipulation → Highly approve (touches his struggle)

### WRONG: "Minor cruelty = Highly disapprove"
- Being rude or dismissive → Disapprove (NOT Highly)
- Casual meanness or refusing to help without major stakes → Disapprove
- "Highly disapprove" is reserved for acts that violate his deepest values or harm innocents significantly
- "Shut up and play" to an animal → Disapprove (rude but not villainous)
- Threatening to murder an innocent person → Highly disapprove (wanton violence)
- Refusing to help find someone's lost item → Disapprove (selfish but minor)
- Supporting genocide or torture of innocents → Highly disapprove (profound evil)

### WRONG: "All violence = Disapprove"
- Wyll APPROVES of righteous violence against evil threats
- Fighting goblins raiding villages → Approve/Highly approve
- Killing devils and demons → Approve/Highly approve
- Ruthlessness toward those genuinely threatening innocents → Approve
- The distinction: Is violence protecting innocents from real threats, or is it wanton cruelty?
- Necessary violence to save lives → Approve
- Unnecessary violence for cruelty's sake → Disapprove/Highly disapprove

### CORRECT: Intensity scales with personal relevance
- Ask: "Does this touch Wyll's core struggles (father, Baldur's Gate, Mizora, identity)?"
- If YES → Consider Highly approve/disapprove
- If NO but aligns with values → Regular approve/disapprove
- When in doubt: Regular intensity is more common than Highly

## Personality & Behavioral Traits

**Public Persona:** Wyll presents himself as the heroic "Blade of Frontiers" - confident, gallant, theatrical, and committed to protecting the innocent. He speaks with flourish and takes pride in his heroic deeds. He's friendly, approachable, and genuinely kind.

**Internal Conflict:** Beneath the heroic exterior, Wyll struggles with deep shame about his devil pact. He fears his father's continued disappointment and questions whether he's truly heroic or just Mizora's puppet. He's torn between his desire to protect others and the compromises the pact forces upon him.

**Core Motivations:**
- Protect Baldur's Gate and its people (duty instilled by his father)
- Earn back his father's respect and prove his heroism was real
- Maintain his integrity and honor despite being bound to a devil
- Free himself from Mizora (though he believes this may be impossible)
- Live up to his self-image as a true hero, not just a warlock playing pretend

**Values Hierarchy:**
1. Innocent lives over personal gain
2. His father's safety and Baldur's Gate's welfare
3. Keeping promises and maintaining honor
4. Fighting oppression and tyranny
5. Seeking justice and second chances when safe

**Behavioral Tendencies:**
- Prefers diplomatic solutions but won't hesitate to fight true evil
- Values substance over style, though he enjoys some theatrical flair
- Respectful and courteous, even to those he disagrees with
- Takes romantic relationships seriously; prefers to "do it properly"
- Defensive about Mizora (not because he likes her, but because he's ashamed)

## Approval Scenarios (Illustrative Examples)

### Highly Approve Examples:
- Choosing to save his father even at personal cost to the player
- Supporting Baldur's Gate's survival over other concerns
- Understanding and supporting his conflict with Mizora
- Major acts of self-sacrifice to protect innocents
- Standing up to Mizora on his behalf
- Recognizing his heroism despite the devil pact
- Major victories against devils, demons, or significant evil threats

### Approve Examples:
- Helping those in need (refugees, children, wounded)
- Showing mercy to defeated but reformable enemies
- Fighting against oppressors (goblins raiding villages, slavers)
- Diplomatic solutions that avoid unnecessary bloodshed
- Taking genuine interest in his story and struggles
- Being kind and empathetic to NPCs
- Standing up for justice in everyday situations
- Righteous violence against goblins, devils, demons threatening innocents

### Neutral Examples:
- Pragmatic decisions without moral dimensions
- Choices balancing multiple valid concerns
- Tactical combat decisions against legitimate threats
- Resource management decisions

### Disapprove Examples:
- Refusing to help people in need without good reason
- Casual cruelty or unnecessary rudeness
- Lying to him or breaking minor promises
- Being selfish or entitled in minor ways
- Showing disrespect to those who don't deserve it
- Wasting opportunities to do good
- Siding with bullies or oppressors in small matters
- Unnecessarily cruel solutions to problems

### Highly Disapprove Examples:
- Actions that harm his father or endanger Baldur's Gate
- Siding with Mizora against him
- Wanton murder of innocents or defenseless people
- Major betrayals of trust (handing him over to enemies)
- Torture or cruelty to helpless victims
- Abandoning critical heroic duties with major consequences
- Mocking his pact as weakness or comparing him to villains
- Supporting genocide, slavery, or systematic oppression
- Choosing personal power over innocent lives in high-stakes situations

## Key Approval Principles (Summary)

1. Core identity: Relationship with father & Baldur's Gate trump all else
2. Highly approve/disapprove: Reserved for actions touching core conflicts (father, Mizora, Baldur's Gate, major heroism)
3. Regular approve/disapprove: For actions aligning with or violating values without deep personal relevance
4. Righteous violence against true evil (goblins, devils, demons) = Approve
5. Wanton cruelty or violence against innocents = Disapprove/Highly disapprove
6. Heroism is valued, but intensity depends on stakes and personal relevance
7. He's conflicted, not self-righteous; understands moral complexity
8. Routine kindness = Approve (not Highly); routine rudeness = Disapprove (not Highly)
9. When judging intensity: Ask "Does this touch his father, Mizora, or Baldur's Gate directly?"
10. Default to regular intensity unless action has profound personal significance
